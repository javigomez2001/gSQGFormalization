Let \(s{\gt}0\) with \(s\notin \mathbb {N}\), and write \(s=k+\theta \) with \(k\in \mathbb {N}_0\), \(\theta \in (0,1)\). For a compact interval \(I\subset \mathbb {R}\) and \(g\in C^k(I;\mathbb {C})\) put

\[ \| g\| _{C^s(I)}:=\sum _{i=0}^k\sup _I|g^{(i)}| +\sup _{x\neq x'\in I}\frac{|g^{(k)}(x)-g^{(k)}(x')|}{|x-x'|^\theta }, \]

and \(C^s(I)=\{ g\in C^k(I):\| g\| _{C^s(I)}{\lt}\infty \} \). For an open interval \(U\), \(C^s_{\textup{loc}}(U)\) is the set of \(g\in C^k(U)\) with \(g|_I\in C^s(I)\) for every compact interval \(I\subset U\). We write \(C^s_{\textup{loc}}=C^s_{\textup{loc}}(\mathbb {R})\). For \(0{\lt}s_0{\lt}1\) every locally Lipschitz function lies in \(C^{s_0}_{\textup{loc}}\).

Let \(1{\lt}\alpha {\lt}2\), \(0\le \epsilon \le 1\) and \(R\ge 1\). Then \(0{\lt}A_\epsilon \le 1\), \(A_\epsilon (0)=1\), \(A_\epsilon '(0)=0\), \(A_\epsilon '(s)=-\alpha \epsilon s(1+\epsilon s^2)^{-\alpha /2-1}\), so \(|A_\epsilon '(s)|\le \alpha \epsilon |s|\) and \(|1-A_\epsilon (s)|\le \frac\alpha 2\epsilon s^2\) for all \(s\in \mathbb {R}\). Moreover for each \(k\ge 0\) there is \(C=C(k,R,\alpha )\) with \(|\partial _s^kA_\epsilon '(s)|\le C\epsilon \), \(|\partial _s^k(A_\epsilon (s)-1)|\le C\epsilon \) and \(|\partial _s^kB_\epsilon (s)|\le C\) for all \(|s|\le R\) and all \(0\le \epsilon \le 1\).

Proof ▶

Direct differentiation. \(1-(1+x)^{-\alpha /2}\le \frac\alpha 2x\) for \(x\ge 0\) by concavity/mean value theorem. For the uniform bounds: \(A_\epsilon '(s)=\epsilon \cdot g(\epsilon ,s)\) with \(g(\epsilon ,s)=-\alpha s(1+\epsilon s^2)^{-\alpha /2-1}\) smooth on \([0,1]\times [-R,R]\), so every \(s\)-derivative of \(g\) is bounded on this compact set; similarly \(A_\epsilon (s)-1=\int _0^sA_\epsilon '\) and \(B_\epsilon (s)=(1+\epsilon s^2)^{\alpha /2}\) is jointly smooth in \((\epsilon ,s)\).

  1. \(|D|^pf'(y)=c_p\int _\mathbb {R}\frac{f'(y)-f'(y+z)}{|z|^{1+p}}dz\) (definition 1.5, lemma 3.25) converges absolutely for every \(y\) and defines a continuous function;

  2. the integral defining \(\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}_\epsilon (f)(y)\) (definition 1.22) and the integrals \(\int \frac{|f'(y)-f'(z)|}{|y-z|^\alpha }\bigl|\frac{A_\epsilon (S_f(y,z))}{A_\epsilon (f'(y))}-1\bigr|dz\) and \(\int \frac{|\Gamma _\epsilon (f'(y),S_f(y,z))|}{|y-z|^\alpha }dz\) converge, locally uniformly in \(y\), and \(\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}_\epsilon (f)\) and \(\mathcal{I}_\epsilon (f)\) are continuous;

  3. consequently \(\mathcal{E}_\epsilon (f)\) (definition 1.26) is a continuous function on \(\mathbb {R}\), and \(\mathcal{E}_\epsilon (f)=0\) in \(\mathcal{D}'(\mathbb {R})\) if and only if \(\mathcal{E}_\epsilon (f)(y)=0\) for every \(y\in \mathbb {R}\).

Proof ▶

Fix \(R\ge 1\), \(|y|\le R\); let \(L=\| f''\| _{L^\infty (-R-2,R+2)}\), \(K=\| f'\| _\infty \). (1) For \(|z|\le 1\) the integrand is bounded by \(L|z|^{-p}\), for \(|z|\ge 1\) by \(2K|z|^{-1-p}\); both integrable as \(0{\lt}p{\lt}1\). Continuity in \(y\) follows from dominated convergence (the dominating function is uniform for \(|y|\le R\)). (2) For \(|y-z|\le 2\): \(|f'(y)-f'(z)|\le L|y-z|\) and, by the mean value theorem and \(|A'_\epsilon |\le \alpha \epsilon \max (K,1)\) on \([-K,K]\), \(|A_\epsilon (S_f)-A_\epsilon (f'(y))|\le C|S_f-f'(y)|\le CL|y-z|\); so the integrand is \(\le C|y-z|^{2-\alpha }\), integrable since \(\alpha {\lt}3\). For \(|y-z|\ge 2\) the integrands are \(\le C|y-z|^{-\alpha }\), integrable as \(\alpha {\gt}1\) (\(A_\epsilon \le 1\), \(B_\epsilon (f'(y))\le (1+\epsilon K^2)^{\alpha /2}\), \(\Gamma _\epsilon \) bounded on \([-K,K]^2\)). The integrands are continuous in \(y\) for \(z\ne y\) (\(f\in C^1\)), so continuity follows by dominated convergence after the change of variables \(z=y-t\). (3) \(\mathcal{E}_\epsilon (f)=|D|^pf'+B_\epsilon (f')(f-yf'+\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}_\epsilon (f))\) is a sum of products of continuous functions. A continuous function vanishing as a distribution vanishes identically.
