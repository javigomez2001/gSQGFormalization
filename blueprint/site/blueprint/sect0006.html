6.1 Standing conventions and elementary tools

Standing assumptions.

Throughout this chapter we fix the parameters of definition 2.1: \(\tfrac 34{\lt}p{\lt}1\), \(\alpha =1+p\), \(\beta =\tfrac {p+1}{2p}\) (so \(2\beta =1+\tfrac 1p\), \(1{\lt}\beta {\lt}\tfrac 1p\) and \(\beta {\lt}1+p\)). \(c_\alpha {\gt}0\) is the constant of definition 1.22 (it depends only on \(p\)) and \(A_\epsilon (u)=(1+\epsilon u^2)^{-\alpha /2}\) (definition 1.10). \(\delta \in (0,|\mu _*|/2]\), \(\mu _*\) (definition 4.15) and \(\overline{B}=\overline{B}(\mu _*,\delta )\) are as in definition 4.38, and \(\epsilon _0\in (0,\epsilon _0^*(\delta )]\), where \(\epsilon _0^*(\delta )\in (0,1]\) is the smallness threshold for \(\epsilon \) provided by proposition 4.49 and the other results of the construction chapter used here (the minimum of the thresholds of proposition 4.49, lemma 4.41, lemma 4.40, lemma 4.47; Lean: c06_eps0_star); every statement of this chapter about the family \(\overline{f}_{\epsilon ,\mu }\) assumes \(\epsilon _0\le \epsilon _0^*(\delta )\); \(R\geq R_0\) is the large parameter of that definition, where \(R_0\geq 1\) is so large that the estimates of lemma 4.33 for \(f_{0,\textup{low}}-W\) hold for \(|y|\geq R_0\) and those of lemma 4.48, proposition 4.49 hold. For \(\epsilon \in [0,\epsilon _0]\), \(\mu \in \overline{B}\) we write \(f=\overline{f}_{\epsilon ,\mu }\), \(Z=Z_{\epsilon ,\mu }\), and \(c_{\textup{cubic}}\), \(c_+\), \(c_\epsilon \), \(f_{0,\textup{low}}\) are as in definition 4.38. \(W(y)=\max \{ -y,0\} \). For \(Y\geq 8R\), the cutoff \(\chi _Y\), the localized front \(v_Y\), the operators \(\mathcal{Q}_\epsilon ,\mathcal{Q}_{\epsilon ,3}\) and the residuals \(\mathcal{E}_6,\dots ,\mathcal{E}_9\) are those of definition 4.51. We assume (as we may) that \(\chi _Y(z)=\chi (z/Y)\) for one fixed \(\chi \in C^\infty _c((1/4,4))\) with \(\chi =1\) on \([1/2,3]\), \(0\le \chi \le 1\); hence \(|\partial ^j\chi _Y|\le C_jY^{-j}\) with \(C_j\) independent of \(Y\). Let \(0{\lt}p{\lt}1\), \(\alpha =1+p\), \(0\leq \epsilon \leq 1\), and define the quintic remainder

\[ \widetilde{A}_\epsilon (u):=c_\alpha \Bigl[A_\epsilon (u)-1+\tfrac {\alpha }{2}\epsilon u^2\Bigr]. \]

There is a constant \(C_A\) depending only on \(p\) such that for all \(u\in \mathbb {R}\):

  1. \(0\leq 1-A_\epsilon (u)\leq \frac\alpha 2\epsilon u^2\);

  2. \(|A_\epsilon '(u)|\leq \alpha \epsilon |u|\);

  3. \(|A_\epsilon ''(u)|+|A_\epsilon '''(u)|\leq C_A\epsilon \);

  4. \(|\widetilde{A}_\epsilon (u)|\leq C_A\epsilon ^2u^4\) and \(|\widetilde{A}_\epsilon '(u)|\leq C_A\epsilon ^2|u|^3\).

In particular \(A_\epsilon (0)=1\), \(A_\epsilon '(0)=0\).

Proof ▶

Write \(A_\epsilon (u)=g(\epsilon u^2)\) with \(g(\sigma )=(1+\sigma )^{-\alpha /2}\), \(\sigma \geq 0\); then \(g^{(k)}(\sigma )=(-1)^k\frac{\alpha }{2}(\frac\alpha 2+1)\cdots (\frac\alpha 2+k-1)(1+\sigma )^{-\alpha /2-k}\), so \(|g^{(k)}(\sigma )|\leq c_k(1+\sigma )^{-\alpha /2-k}\) with \(c_1=\alpha /2\). (1) is \(|g(\sigma )-g(0)|\leq \sup |g'|\, \sigma \) and \(g\) decreasing. (2): \(A_\epsilon '(u)=2\epsilon u\, g'(\epsilon u^2)\) and \(|g'|\leq \alpha /2\). (3): \(A_\epsilon ''=2\epsilon g'+4\epsilon ^2u^2g''\) and \(A_\epsilon '''=12\epsilon ^2u\, g''+8\epsilon ^3u^3g'''\), all evaluated at \(\epsilon u^2\). Put \(x=\sqrt\epsilon \, u\); then \(\epsilon ^2u^2|g''|=\epsilon \, x^2(1+x^2)^{-\alpha /2-2}c_2\leq c_2\epsilon \), \(\epsilon ^2|u||g''|=\epsilon ^{3/2}|x|(1+x^2)^{-\alpha /2-2}c_2\leq c_2\epsilon \) and \(\epsilon ^3|u|^3|g'''|\leq c_3\epsilon ^{3/2}\leq c_3\epsilon \) (using \(\epsilon \le 1\)). (4): \(\widetilde{A}_\epsilon (u)=c_\alpha [g(\sigma )-g(0)-g'(0)\sigma ]\) with \(\sigma =\epsilon u^2\) (note \(g'(0)=-\alpha /2\)), so Taylor’s formula gives \(|\widetilde{A}_\epsilon (u)|\leq \frac{c_\alpha c_2}{2}\epsilon ^2u^4\). Also \(\widetilde{A}_\epsilon '(u)=2c_\alpha \epsilon u\, [g'(\epsilon u^2)-g'(0)]\) and \(|g'(\sigma )-g'(0)|\leq c_2\sigma \).

Let \(w\in C^1(\mathbb {R})\) with \(\sup |w'|{\lt}\infty \). Then:

  1. \(|S_w(y,z)|\leq \sup _{\mathbb {R}}|w'|\) for all \(y\neq z\); more precisely \(|S_w(y,z)|\le \sup _{I}|w'|\) where \(I\) is the segment between \(y\) and \(z\);

  2. if moreover \(w\in C^2(\mathbb {R})\) with \(\sup |w''|{\lt}\infty \), then for every \(s\in \mathbb {R}\) the map \(y\mapsto S_w(y,y-s)\) is \(C^1\) with \(\partial _yS_w(y,y-s)=S_{w'}(y,y-s)\), and \(|S_{w'}(y,y-s)|\leq \sup |w''|\);

  3. for fixed \(z\) and \(y\neq z\), \(\partial _yS_w(y,z)=\dfrac {w’(y)-S_w(y,z)}{y-z}\);

  4. \(S_{w_1+w_2}=S_{w_1}+S_{w_2}\) and \(S_{cw}=cS_w\) for \(c\in \mathbb {R}\).

Proof ▶

(1) mean value theorem. (2) differentiate \(S_w(y,y-s)=\int _0^1w'(y-\theta s)\, d\theta \) under the integral (the integrand’s \(y\)-derivative \(w''(y-\theta s)\) is bounded), obtaining \(\int _0^1w''(y-\theta s)\, d\theta =S_{w'}(y,y-s)\). (3) quotient rule. (4) linearity.

Let \(0{\lt}p{\lt}1\), \(A,B{\gt}0\), and let \(w:\mathbb {R}\to \mathbb {R}\) satisfy \(|w|\leq A\) and \(|w(a)-w(b)|\leq B|a-b|\) for all \(a,b\). Then for every \(y\in \mathbb {R}\)

\[ \int _\mathbb {R}\frac{|w(y)-w(y-s)|}{|s|^{1+p}}\, ds\leq C_pA^{1-p}B^p,\qquad C_p=2^{2-p}\Bigl(\frac1{1-p}+\frac1p\Bigr). \]
Proof ▶

\(|w(y)-w(y-s)|\leq \min \{ 2A,B|s|\} \). Put \(r=2A/B\). Then \(\int _{|s|{\lt}r}B|s|^{-p}\, ds=\frac{2B r^{1-p}}{1-p}=\frac{2^{2-p}}{1-p}A^{1-p}B^p\) and \(\int _{|s|\geq r}2A|s|^{-1-p}\, ds=\frac{4Ar^{-p}}{p}=\frac{2^{2-p}}{p}A^{1-p}B^p\).

Let \(0{\lt}p{\lt}1\) and \(w\in C^3(\mathbb {R})\) with \(\sup |w^{(k)}|\leq B_k{\lt}\infty \), \(k=1,2,3\) (\(B_k{\gt}0\)). Let \(\Phi :\mathbb {R}\times \mathbb {R}\to \mathbb {R}\) be measurable, such that for every \(s\) the map \(y\mapsto \Phi (y,s)\) is differentiable, \(y\mapsto \partial _y\Phi (y,s)\) is measurable in \((y,s)\), and \(|\Phi |\leq M_0\), \(|\partial _y\Phi |\leq M_1\) on \(\mathbb {R}\times \mathbb {R}\). Set

\[ I(y)=\int _\mathbb {R}\frac{w'(y)-w'(y-s)}{|s|^{1+p}}\Phi (y,s)\, ds . \]

Then the integral converges absolutely for every \(y\), \(I\) is differentiable on \(\mathbb {R}\),

\[ I'(y)=\int _\mathbb {R}\frac{(w''(y)-w''(y-s))\Phi (y,s)+(w'(y)-w'(y-s))\partial _y\Phi (y,s)}{|s|^{1+p}}\, ds, \]

and, with \(C_p\) as in lemma 6.3,

\[ |I(y)|\leq C_pM_0B_1^{1-p}B_2^p,\qquad |I'(y)|\leq C_p\bigl(M_0B_2^{1-p}B_3^p+M_1B_1^{1-p}B_2^p\bigr). \]
Proof ▶

Absolute convergence and the bound for \(I\): apply lemma 6.3 to \(w'\) (\(A=B_1\), \(B=B_2\)). For differentiability use the dominated differentiation theorem (Mathlib: hasDerivAt_integral_of_dominated_loc_of_deriv_le): the \(y\)-derivative of the integrand is bounded, uniformly in \(y\), by \(\bigl(M_0\min \{ 2B_2,B_3|s|\} +M_1\min \{ 2B_1,B_2|s|\} \bigr)|s|^{-1-p}\), which is integrable in \(s\) by the computation in lemma 6.3; the integrand at a base point is integrable likewise. The bound for \(I'\) follows by applying lemma 6.3 to \(w''\) (\(A=B_2\), \(B=B_3\)) and to \(w'\).

Let \(0{\lt}p{\lt}1\).

  1. For \(a{\gt}1\) and \(y{\gt}0\): \(\int _{-\infty }^0(y-z)^{-a}\, dz=\frac{y^{1-a}}{a-1}\).

  2. For \(a{\gt}1\), \(r{\gt}0\) and \(y\in \mathbb {R}\): \(\int _{|y-z|\geq r}|y-z|^{-a}\, dz=\frac{2r^{1-a}}{a-1}\).

  3. For \(a{\gt}0\) and \(y{\gt}0\): \(\int _{-\infty }^0\langle z \rangle ^{-1-p}(y-z)^{-a}\, dz\leq (1+\frac1p)\, y^{-a}\).

  4. For \(a{\gt}1-p\) and \(y{\gt}0\): \(\int _0^\infty \langle t \rangle ^{-p}(y+t)^{-a}\, dt\leq \bigl(\frac1{1-p}+\frac1{a+p-1}\bigr)y^{1-p-a}\).

Proof ▶

(1),(2) direct integration. (3) \((y-z)^{-a}\leq y^{-a}\) for \(z\leq 0\) and \(\int _{-\infty }^0\langle z \rangle ^{-1-p}\, dz\leq 1+\int _1^\infty z^{-1-p}\, dz=1+\frac1p\). (4) On \([0,y]\) use \((y+t)^{-a}\leq y^{-a}\) and \(\langle t \rangle ^{-p}\leq t^{-p}\), giving \(y^{-a}\frac{y^{1-p}}{1-p}\); on \([y,\infty )\) use \(\langle t \rangle ^{-p}(y+t)^{-a}\leq t^{-p-a}\), giving \(\frac{y^{1-p-a}}{a+p-1}\).
