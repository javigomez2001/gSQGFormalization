9.1 Standing conventions

Throughout this chapter \(p,\alpha =p+1,\beta =\frac{p+1}{2p}\) are as in definition 2.1 (\(\frac34{\lt}p{\lt}1\)), and \(\gamma \) is called admissible if \(\beta +\frac1p{\lt}\gamma {\lt}2p+1\). Note \(\beta +\frac1p=\frac{p+3}{2p}\), so admissible \(\gamma \) satisfy \(\gamma -\frac1p{\gt}\beta {\gt}1\). \(\Phi _0(\xi )=\xi |\xi |^p/\alpha \) (so \(\Phi _0'(\xi )=|\xi |^p\)). The Fourier transform is \(\widehat f(\xi )=\int _\mathbb {R}f(x)e^{-ix\xi }\, dx\) (definition 1.2) and for an integrable symbol \(m\) we write \(\check{m}(z)=\frac1{2\pi }\int _\mathbb {R}m(\xi )e^{iz\xi }\, d\xi \). \(m_G\) is the symbol of \(\mathcal{T}_0\) (definition 8.33), so \(\widehat{\mathcal{T}_0G}=m_G\) in \(\mathcal S'\). \(\chi \in C^\infty _c(\mathbb {R};[0,1])\) is the fixed even cutoff of definition 8.42 (\(\chi =1\) on \([-1,1]\), \(\operatorname {supp}\chi \subset [-2,2]\)), and \(\mathcal{T}_{\textup{l}},\mathcal{T}_{\textup{h}}\) are the low/high frequency parts of \(\mathcal{T}_0\) defined there: \(\widehat{\mathcal{T}_{\textup{l}}G}=\chi \, m_G\), \(\widehat{\mathcal{T}_{\textup{h}}G}=(1-\chi )m_G\) (??). Unless said otherwise, “\(C\)” denotes a constant depending only on \(p\), \(\gamma \) and \(\chi \) (and on the further parameters \(M,L,k,\ell \) when indicated by a subscript); it never depends on \(y,z,\xi ,R\) or \(G\).

Let \(w_0,w_1,w_2:\mathbb {R}\to (0,\infty )\) be continuous, \(A\ge 0\), and let \((r_R)_{R\ge 2}\) be real \(C^2\) functions with \(r_R\to r\) uniformly on \(\mathbb {R}\) as \(R\to \infty \). Proof ▶

Fix \(Y\) and a sequence \(R_n\to \infty \), \(R_n\ge R_1(Y)\). On \([-Y,Y]\) the \(r'_{R_n}\) are uniformly bounded and uniformly Lipschitz (\(|r''_{R_n}|\le A\max w_2\)), so by Arzelà–Ascoli a subsequence converges uniformly to some \(g\).
