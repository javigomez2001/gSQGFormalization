1 Introduction and setup: self-similar reduction and Theorem 1.1 (paper §§1–2)

Throughout this chapter \(\alpha \in (1,2)\) is fixed and we write \(p=\alpha -1\in (0,1)\). Unless said otherwise, all functions are real valued. The Japanese bracket is \(\langle y \rangle =(1+y^2)^{1/2}\). We set

\[ c_\alpha =\frac{\Gamma (\alpha )\sin (\pi (\alpha -1)/2)}{\pi }, \]

which is a strictly positive real number for \(\alpha \in (1,2)\) (since \(\Gamma (\alpha ){\gt}0\) and \(0{\lt}\pi p/2{\lt}\pi /2\)). We write \(W(y)=y_-=\max \{ -y,0\} \) for the model corner.

For \(f\in L^1(\mathbb {R};\mathbb {C})\) the Fourier transform is

\[ \widehat f(\xi )=\int _{-\infty }^{\infty } f(x)e^{-ix\xi }\, dx ,\qquad \xi \in \mathbb {R}, \]

extended to tempered distributions \(\mathcal S'(\mathbb {R})\) by duality, \(\langle \widehat u,\varphi \rangle =\langle u,\widehat\varphi \rangle \). The inverse transform is \(\check{g}(x)=\frac1{2\pi }\int _\mathbb {R}g(\xi )e^{ix\xi }\, d\xi \). With this convention \(\widehat{\partial _x f}(\xi )=i\xi \widehat f(\xi )\). A Fourier multiplier with symbol \(m\in L^\infty _{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb {R})\) of polynomial growth acts on \(f\in \mathcal S(\mathbb {R})\) by \(m(D)f=(m\widehat f)^{\vee }\). (Mathlib’s Real.fourierIntegral uses \(e^{-2\pi i x\xi }\); the two conventions differ by the substitution \(\xi \mapsto 2\pi \xi \).)

\(\mathcal A\) denotes the set of \(f\in C^1(\mathbb {R})\) such that \(f'\in L^\infty (\mathbb {R})\) and \(f'\) is locally Lipschitz. Let \(0{\lt}p{\lt}1\), \(\alpha =p+1\).

  1. For \(g\in L^\infty (\mathbb {R})\) which is locally Lipschitz, define pointwise for every \(y\in \mathbb {R}\)

    \[ |D|^{p}g(y):=c_\alpha \int _\mathbb {R}\frac{g(y)-g(z)}{|y-z|^{\alpha }}\, dz . \]

    (The integral is absolutely convergent by lemma 1.6; no principal value is needed because \(\alpha {\lt}2\).)

  2. For \(f\in \mathcal A\) define \(|D|^{p}\partial _y f:=|D|^p(f')\), i.e.

    \[ |D|^{\alpha -1}\partial _y f(y)=c_\alpha \int _\mathbb {R}\frac{f'(y)-f'(z)}{|y-z|^{\alpha }}\, dz . \]

    This is the meaning of \(|D|^{\alpha -1}\partial _y f\) for non-decaying \(f\) (e.g. \(f'\to -1\) at \(-\infty \)): only \(f'\) enters, and \(f'\) is bounded.

  3. (Fourier side.) For \(f\in \mathcal S(\mathbb {R})\), \(|D|^p f=(|\xi |^{p}\widehat f)^\vee \) and \(|D|^{p}\partial _y f=(i\xi |\xi |^{p}\widehat f)^\vee \); the two definitions agree on \(\mathcal S(\mathbb {R})\) by lemma 1.8.

Let \(0{\lt}p{\lt}1\), \(\alpha =p+1\), and \(g\in L^\infty (\mathbb {R})\) be Lipschitz on \([y-1,y+1]\) with constant \(\Lambda _y\). Then \(z\mapsto (g(y)-g(z))|y-z|^{-\alpha }\) is in \(L^1(\mathbb {R})\) and

\[ \bigl||D|^pg(y)\bigr|\le c_\alpha \Bigl(\frac{2\Lambda _y}{2-\alpha } +\frac{4\| g\| _{L^\infty }}{\alpha -1}\Bigr). \]
Proof ▶

Split \(\mathbb {R}=\{ |z-y|\le 1\} \cup \{ |z-y|{\gt}1\} \). On the first set \(|g(y)-g(z)|\, |y-z|^{-\alpha }\le \Lambda _y|y-z|^{1-\alpha }\), and \(\int _{|h|\le 1}|h|^{1-\alpha }dh=2/(2-\alpha )\) since \(1-\alpha {\gt}-1\). On the second set the integrand is bounded by \(2\| g\| _\infty |y-z|^{-\alpha }\) and \(\int _{|h|{\gt}1}|h|^{-\alpha }dh=2/(\alpha -1)\) since \(\alpha {\gt}1\).

Let \(0{\lt}p{\lt}1\), \(\alpha =p+1\). For every \(g\in \mathcal S(\mathbb {R})\) and \(y\in \mathbb {R}\),

\[ (|\xi |^p\widehat g)^\vee (y)=c_\alpha \int _\mathbb {R}\frac{g(y)-g(z)}{|y-z|^{\alpha }}\, dz, \qquad c_\alpha =\frac{\Gamma (1+p)\sin (\pi p/2)}{\pi }. \]

Consequently, for \(f\in \mathcal S(\mathbb {R})\), \(|D|^p\partial _y f=(i\xi |\xi |^p\widehat f)^\vee \), and the linearization at \(0\) of the gSQG nonlinearity is \(|D|^{\alpha -1}\partial _x\) (see lemma 1.15).

Proof ▶

Write \(z=y+h\) and symmetrize (\(h\mapsto -h\), allowed since the integral converges absolutely by lemma 1.6):

\[ c_\alpha \int _\mathbb {R}\frac{g(y)-g(y+h)}{|h|^{\alpha }}dh =\frac{c_\alpha }2\int _\mathbb {R}\frac{2g(y)-g(y+h)-g(y-h)}{|h|^{\alpha }}dh . \]

Insert \(g(x)=\frac1{2\pi }\int \widehat g(\xi )e^{ix\xi }d\xi \): \(2g(y)-g(y+h)-g(y-h)=\frac1{2\pi }\int \widehat g(\xi )e^{iy\xi }\, 2(1-\cos h\xi )\, d\xi \). Fubini is justified since \(|\widehat g(\xi )|\, (1-\cos h\xi )|h|^{-\alpha } \le |\widehat g(\xi )|\min (h^2\xi ^2,2)|h|^{-\alpha }\), which is integrable on \(\mathbb {R}^2\) (\(\widehat g\) is Schwartz; \(\int \min (h^2\xi ^2,2)|h|^{-\alpha }dh=C|\xi |^{p}\)). Substituting \(s=|h\xi |\),

\[ \int _\mathbb {R}\frac{1-\cos h\xi }{|h|^{1+p}}dh=2|\xi |^p\int _0^\infty \frac{1-\cos s}{s^{1+p}}ds =2|\xi |^p\, \frac{\Gamma (1-p)\cos (\pi p/2)}{p}. \]

The last integral is classical (integrate by parts once to get \(\frac1p\int _0^\infty s^{-p}\sin s\, ds=\frac1p\Gamma (1-p)\sin (\pi (1-p)/2)\)). Hence the right side equals \(c_\alpha \frac{2\Gamma (1-p)\cos (\pi p/2)}{p}(|\xi |^p\widehat g)^\vee (y)\), and by \(\Gamma (p)\Gamma (1-p)=\pi /\sin (\pi p)\) and \(\sin (\pi p)=2\sin (\pi p/2)\cos (\pi p/2)\),

\[ \frac{p}{2\Gamma (1-p)\cos (\pi p/2)}=\frac{p\Gamma (p)\sin (\pi p)}{2\pi \cos (\pi p/2)} =\frac{\Gamma (1+p)\sin (\pi p/2)}{\pi }=c_\alpha . \]

For the second claim apply the first to \(g=f'\) and use \(\widehat{f'}=i\xi \widehat f\).

Let \(g\in L^\infty (\mathbb {R})\cap W^{1,\infty }_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb {R})\) with \(|g'(y)|\le C\langle y \rangle ^N\) a.e. for some \(C,N\ge 0\). Then \(|D|^pg\) (pointwise, definition 1.5) is continuous, satisfies \(\bigl||D|^pg(y)\bigr|\le C'\langle y \rangle ^N\), and for every \(\varphi \in \mathcal S(\mathbb {R})\)

\[ \int _\mathbb {R}|D|^pg(y)\, \varphi (y)\, dy=\int _\mathbb {R}g(y)\, |D|^p\varphi (y)\, dy , \]

where \(|D|^p\varphi =(|\xi |^p\widehat\varphi )^\vee \) satisfies \(|\, |D|^p\varphi (y)|\le C_\varphi \langle y \rangle ^{-\alpha }\). In particular the pointwise \(|D|^pg\) coincides with the distribution \(\varphi \mapsto \langle g,|D|^p\varphi \rangle \), so a pointwise identity \(\mathcal{E}_\epsilon (f)=0\) on \(\mathbb {R}\) (definition 1.26) for \(f\in \mathcal A\) with \(f''\) of polynomial growth implies \(\mathcal{E}_\epsilon (f)=0\) in \(\mathcal D'(\mathbb {R})\).

Proof ▶

The pointwise bound follows from lemma 1.6 with \(\Lambda _y\le C\langle |y|+1 \rangle ^N\le C2^N\langle y \rangle ^N\). Continuity: dominated convergence in the representation \(c_\alpha \int (g(y)-g(y+h))|h|^{-\alpha }dh\), with dominating function \(\Lambda |h|^{1-\alpha }\mathbf1_{|h|\le 1}+2\| g\| _\infty |h|^{-\alpha }\mathbf1_{|h|{\gt}1}\) uniform for \(y\) in compacts. The decay of \(|D|^p\varphi \): by lemma 1.8, \(|D|^p\varphi (y)=c_\alpha \int (\varphi (y)-\varphi (z))|y-z|^{-\alpha }dz\); for \(|y|\ge 2\) split \(|z-y|{\lt}|y|/2\) (use \(|\varphi (y)-\varphi (z)|\le |y-z|\sup _{|w|\ge |y|/2}|\varphi '(w)|\), rapidly decaying) and \(|z-y|\ge |y|/2\) (bounded by \(\| \varphi \| _\infty \int _{|h|\ge |y|/2}|h|^{-\alpha }dh +(|y|/2)^{-\alpha }\| \varphi \| _{L^1}\)). Duality: for \(\delta {\gt}0\) put \(K_\delta (y,z)=c_\alpha |y-z|^{-\alpha }\mathbf1_{|y-z|{\gt}\delta }\). Then \(\iint |g(y)-g(z)||\varphi (y)|K_\delta \, dz\, dy{\lt}\infty \) and likewise with \(\varphi (z)\), so

\[ \iint (g(y)-g(z))\varphi (y)K_\delta =\tfrac 12\iint (g(y)-g(z))(\varphi (y)-\varphi (z))K_\delta =\iint g(y)(\varphi (y)-\varphi (z))K_\delta \]

(swap \(y\leftrightarrow z\), symmetry of \(K_\delta \)). Let \(\delta \to 0\): on the left the inner integral converges to \(|D|^pg(y)\) and is dominated by \(C'\langle y \rangle ^N\), which is integrable against \(|\varphi |\); on the right the inner integral converges to \(|D|^p\varphi (y)\), dominated by \(C_\varphi \langle y \rangle ^{-\alpha }\), integrable against \(|g|\le \| g\| _\infty \). For the last statement apply this with \(g=f'\); the other terms of \(\mathcal{E}_\epsilon (f)\) are continuous functions.
