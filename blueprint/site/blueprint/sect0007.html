7.1 Standing conventions and auxiliary objects

Throughout this chapter we use the following standing assumptions.

  • \(p,\beta \) are as in definition 2.1: \(\tfrac 34{\lt}p{\lt}1\), \(\alpha =p+1\), \(\beta =\tfrac {p+1}{2p}\). Note \(\beta {\gt}1\) (since \(p{\lt}1\)). We write \(\theta :=\tfrac 1p-\beta =\tfrac {1-p}{2p}\); since \(p{\gt}\tfrac 12\) we have \(0{\lt}\theta {\lt}p\).

  • \(c_\alpha =\Gamma (\alpha )\sin (\pi (\alpha -1)/2)/\pi {\gt}0\) is the constant of definition 1.5, so that for \(g\) bounded and locally Lipschitz \(|D|^pg(y)=c_\alpha \int _\mathbb {R}\frac{g(y)-g(z)}{|y-z|^{1+p}}\, dz\) (absolutely convergent).

  • \(A_\epsilon (u)=(1+\epsilon u^2)^{-\alpha /2}\) (definition 1.10) and \(B_\epsilon (u)=A_\epsilon (u)^{-1}=(1+\epsilon u^2)^{\alpha /2}\).

  • \(\mu _*=-i/\sqrt{2\pi p}\) (definition 4.15); the parameters \(R\geq 1\), \(\epsilon _0\in (0,1]\), \(\delta {\gt}0\), the cutoffs \(\chi _+,\chi _R\), the closed ball \(\overline{B}=\{ \mu \in \mathbb {C}:|\mu -\mu _*|\le \delta \} \), and the family \(\overline{f}_{\epsilon ,\mu }\) with its pieces \(f_{0,\mu },f_{0,+},f_{\textup{slow}},f_{\textup{osc}},Z_{\epsilon ,\mu },\lambda _{\epsilon ,\mu },\Psi _{\epsilon ,\mu }\) and constants \(c_\pm ,c_{\textup{cubic}},n_\epsilon ,c_{\textup{log}}(\mu )\) are those of definition 4.38. Here \(R\) is large and \(\epsilon _0\) small, chosen in Chapter 4 so that ?? hold; we additionally use that \(\epsilon _0\) is small enough that \(\lambda _{\epsilon ,\mu }(t)\ge \tfrac 12 t^{1/p}\) for \(t\ge R\) (this follows from ?? with \(j=0\)).

  • \(\chi \in C_c^\infty ((-\tfrac 12,\tfrac 12))\) is a fixed even cutoff with \(0\le \chi \le 1\) and \(\chi =1\) on \([-\tfrac 14,\tfrac 14]\).

  • A constant is called admissible if it depends only on \(p\), \(R\), \(\delta \) and the fixed cutoffs \(\chi _+,\chi _R,\chi \) (and, when indicated, on \(\gamma \), \(j\), \(M\), \(K\)); it never depends on \(\epsilon \in [0,\epsilon _0]\), \(\mu \in \overline{B}\), the point \(y\), or a sequence index \(n\).

  • For \(M{\gt}0\), \(k\geq 0\), \(\| u\| _{C^k([-M,M])}:=\sum _{i\le k}\sup _{|y|\le M}|u^{(i)}(y)|\). A sequence \(u_n\to u\) in \(C^k_{\textup{loc}}\) means \(\| u_n-u\| _{C^k([-M,M])}\to 0\) for every \(M{\gt}0\); a map \(\mu \mapsto u_\mu \) from \(\overline{B}\) is continuous into \(C^k_{\textup{loc}}\) if \(\mu _n\to \mu \) in \(\overline{B}\) implies \(u_{\mu _n}\to u_\mu \) in \(C^k_{\textup{loc}}\) (\(\overline{B}\) is a metric space, so sequential continuity suffices).

There is \(C=C(p)\) such that for all \(0\le \epsilon \le 1\) and \(u\in \mathbb {R}\):

\[ |A_\epsilon (u)-1|\le \tfrac \alpha 2\epsilon u^2,\quad |A_\epsilon '(u)|\le \alpha \epsilon |u|,\quad |A_\epsilon ''(u)|\le C\epsilon ,\quad 0\le B_\epsilon (u)-1\le \tfrac \alpha 2\epsilon u^2,\quad |B_\epsilon '(u)|\le \alpha \epsilon |u|. \]

In particular, for \(K\ge 1\), \(\| A_\epsilon -1\| _K+\| A_\epsilon '\| _K\le C K^2\epsilon \) and \(\| A_\epsilon \| _K\le 1+CK^2\).

Proof ▶

Put \(x=\epsilon u^2\ge 0\). Since \(x\mapsto (1+x)^{-\alpha /2}\) is convex with derivative \(-\alpha /2\) at \(0\), \(0\le 1-(1+x)^{-\alpha /2}\le \frac\alpha 2x\). Since \(\alpha /2{\lt}1\), \(x\mapsto (1+x)^{\alpha /2}\) is concave, so \(0\le (1+x)^{\alpha /2}-1\le \frac\alpha 2x\). Directly, \(A_\epsilon '(u)=-\alpha \epsilon u(1+\epsilon u^2)^{-\alpha /2-1}\), \(B_\epsilon '(u)=\alpha \epsilon u(1+\epsilon u^2)^{\alpha /2-1}\), and both factors \((1+x)^{-\alpha /2-1},(1+x)^{\alpha /2-1}\) are \(\le 1\). Finally \(A_\epsilon ''(u)=-\alpha \epsilon (1+x)^{-\alpha /2-1}+\alpha (\alpha +2)\epsilon \frac{x}{1+x}(1+x)^{-\alpha /2-1}\), so \(|A_\epsilon ''|\le \alpha (\alpha +3)\epsilon \).

Let \(f\in C^1(\mathbb {R})\) with \(f'\) bounded. For \(y\ne z\): \(|S_f(y,z)|\le \| f'\| _{L^\infty }\), and

\[ \partial _yS_f=\frac{f'(y)-S_f}{y-z},\qquad \partial _zS_f=\frac{S_f-f'(z)}{y-z},\qquad \partial _y\partial _zS_f=\frac{f'(y)+f'(z)-2S_f}{(y-z)^2}. \]

Moreover \(S_f(y,y-s)=\int _0^1f'(y-ts)\, dt\), \(\partial _y[S_f(y,y-s)]=\frac{f'(y)-f'(y-s)}{s}\), and \(S_f-S_g=S_{f-g}\) (linearity). If \(f\) is only continuous and piecewise \(C^1\) with bounded derivative (e.g. \(f=W\)) the same holds wherever the derivatives exist.

Proof ▶

Mean value theorem / fundamental theorem of calculus for the first claims, quotient rule for the derivatives: e.g. \(\partial _zS_f=\frac{-f'(z)(y-z)+(f(y)-f(z))}{(y-z)^2}\); then \(\partial _y\partial _zS_f=\frac{\partial _yS_f}{y-z}-\frac{S_f-f'(z)}{(y-z)^2}\) and insert \(\partial _yS_f\).

Then for every \(y\in \mathbb {R}\) the integrals defining \(|D|^pf'(y)\), \(\mathcal{Q}_\epsilon (f)(y)\), \(\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}_\epsilon (f)(y)\) and \(\mathcal{T}_{A_\epsilon }(f)(y)\) converge absolutely, and

\[ \widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_\epsilon (f)=\mathcal{L}_0(f)+\mathcal{Q}_\epsilon (f)=f-yf'+\mathcal{T}_{A_\epsilon }(f), \qquad \mathcal{E}_\epsilon (f)=B_\epsilon (f')\, \widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_\epsilon (f). \]
Proof ▶

Fix \(y\). For \(|y-z|\le 1\), \(|f'(y)-f'(z)|\le |y-z|\sup _{[y-1,y+1]}|f''|\), so each integrand is \(O(|y-z|^{1-\alpha })=O(|y-z|^{-p})\), integrable; for \(|y-z|{\gt}1\) each integrand is \(\le 2\| f'\| _\infty (1+\sup |A_\epsilon |)|y-z|^{-\alpha }\), integrable since \(\alpha {\gt}1\) (and \(0{\lt}A_\epsilon \le 1\)). Let \(K\ge 1\), \(f\in \mathcal{P}(K)\) and \(\Gamma \in C^1(\mathbb {R};\mathbb {R})\). Then for every \(y\) the integral defining \(\mathcal{T}_\Gamma (f)(y)\) converges absolutely, \(\mathcal{T}_\Gamma (f)\in C^1(\mathbb {R})\), and

\[ \partial _y\mathcal{T}_\Gamma (f)(y)=c_\alpha \int _\mathbb {R}\Bigl[\frac{f''(y)-f''(y-s)}{|s|^\alpha }\Gamma (S) +\frac{(f'(y)-f'(y-s))^2}{s|s|^\alpha }\Gamma '(S)\Bigr]ds,\qquad S=S_f(y,y-s), \]

Write \(k(y,s)\) for the integrand of \(\mathcal{T}_\Gamma (f)\) and \(k_1(y,s)\) for the integrand above. By lemma 7.5, \(|S|\le K\) and \(\partial _yk=k_1\) for \(s\neq 0\) (chain rule, using \(\partial _y S_f(y,y-s)=(f'(y)-f'(y-s))/s\)). Fix \(M\ge 1\) and let \(|y|\le M+1\). Set \(K_2:=K\langle M+2 \rangle ^{1/p}\ge \sup _{|x|\le M+2}|f''(x)|\) and \(K_3:=K\langle M+2 \rangle ^{2/p}\ge \sup _{|x|\le M+2}|f'''(x)|\). Let \(D_M(s)\) be the resulting majorant (independent of \(y\in [-M-1,M+1]\)). Since \(p{\lt}1\) and \(\theta {\lt}p\) (here \(p{\gt}\tfrac 12\) is used), \(D_M\in L^1(\mathbb {R})\) with \(\| D_M\| _{L^1}\le C(p,K,M)\| \Gamma \| _K\). For each \(s\ne 0\), \(y\mapsto k(y,s)\) is \(C^1\) with derivative \(k_1\) and \(|k_1(y,s)|\le D_M(s)\) for \(|y|{\lt}M+1\); the theorem on differentiation under the integral sign (dominated derivative; Mathlib hasDerivAt_integral_of_dominated_loc_of_deriv_le) gives differentiability with the stated formula.
