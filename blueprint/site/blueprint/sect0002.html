\[ \frac34 {\lt} p {\lt} 1,\qquad \alpha = p+1,\qquad \beta = \frac{p+1}{2p},\qquad \beta +\frac1p {\lt} \gamma _1 {\lt} \gamma _2 {\lt} 2p+1 . \]

Lemma 2.5 \(X_\gamma \) is a Banach space
✓
#

Uses

  • Definition 2.3

Lean declarations

  • GSQG.c02_X_gamma_banach

For every \(\gamma \in \mathbb {R}\), \((X_\gamma ,\| \cdot \| _{X_\gamma })\) is a real Banach space.

Proof ▶

Norm axioms are immediate (definiteness: \(\| r\| _{X_\gamma }=0\) forces \(r=0\) a.e., hence everywhere by continuity). Put weights \(w_0=\langle y \rangle ^\gamma \), and \(w_1=\langle y \rangle ^{\gamma -1/p}\), \(w_2=\langle y \rangle ^{\gamma -2/p}\) on \(y\ge 0\), \(w_1=\langle y \rangle ^{\gamma +1}\), \(w_2=\langle y \rangle ^{\gamma +2}\) on \(y{\lt}0\). Then \(\frac12\max _k\| w_kr^{(k)}\| _{L^\infty }\le \| r\| _{X_\gamma }\le 6\max _k\| w_kr^{(k)}\| _{L^\infty }\), so it suffices to show completeness for the equivalent norm \(\max _k\| w_kr^{(k)}\| _{L^\infty }\). Let \((r_n)\) be Cauchy. Then \(w_kr_n^{(k)}\to h_k\) in \(L^\infty (\mathbb {R})\) for \(k=0,1,2\). On each compact \([a,b]\) the weights are bounded above and below by positive constants, so \(r_n\to g_0:=h_0/w_0\) and \(r_n'\to g_1:=h_1/w_1\) uniformly on \([a,b]\) (these are uniform limits of continuous functions, so \(g_0,g_1\) are continuous after modification on a null set; in fact the convergence is uniform on compacts for every point since \(r_n,r_n'\) are continuous and Cauchy in sup on compacts), and \(r_n''\to g_2:=h_2/w_2\) in \(L^\infty ([a,b])\). Passing to the limit in \(r_n(y)-r_n(a)=\int _a^yr_n'\) and \(r_n'(y)-r_n'(a)=\int _a^y r_n''\) (FTC for \(C^1\) functions, resp. for Lipschitz functions) gives \(g_0\in C^1\) with \(g_0'=g_1\), and \(g_1(y)-g_1(a)=\int _a^yg_2\); hence \(g_1\) is locally Lipschitz with \(g_1'=g_2\) a.e. Thus \(r:=g_0\in W^{2,\infty }_{\mathrm{loc}}\), \(w_kr^{(k)}=h_k\) a.e., so \(r\in X_\gamma \) and \(r_n\to r\) in \(X_\gamma \).

Write \(z(\mu ):=1-\mu /\mu _*\). Then for all \(\mu \in \mathbb {C}\):

  1. \(G_\mu =\operatorname {Re}(z(\mu ))\, G_++\operatorname {Im}(z(\mu ))\, G_-\); in particular \(G_\mu \) is a real Schwartz function and \(G_{\mu _*}=0\);

  2. \(\ell (G_\mu )=z(\mu )\);

  3. for every \(\gamma \), \(\mu \mapsto G_\mu \) is a continuous (\(\mathbb {R}\)-affine) map \(\mathbb {C}\to Y_\gamma \), and \(\sup _{|\mu -\mu _*|\le \delta }\| G_\mu \| _{Y_\gamma }\le \frac{\delta }{|\mu _*|}(\| G_+\| _{Y_\gamma }+\| G_-\| _{Y_\gamma })\) for any \(\delta {\gt}0\).

  1. \(\widetilde{Y}_\gamma \) is a closed \(\mathbb {R}\)-linear subspace of \(Y_\gamma \) (hence a Banach space);

Lemma 2.41 Brouwer fixed point theorem for a closed disc in \(\mathbb {C}\)
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • GSQG.c02_brouwer_disc

Let \(c\in \mathbb {C}\), \(\delta {\gt}0\), \(\overline{B}=\{ \mu :|\mu -c|\le \delta \} \) and \(g:\overline{B}\to \overline{B}\) continuous. Then \(g\) has a fixed point.

Proof ▶

By the affine homeomorphism \(\mu \mapsto (\mu -c)/\delta \) reduce to the closed unit disc \(\overline{\mathbb D}\). If \(g\) had no fixed point, the map \(\rho (\mu ):=\) the intersection of the ray from \(g(\mu )\) through \(\mu \) with \(\partial \mathbb D\) (explicitly, \(\rho (\mu )=\mu +t(\mu )(\mu -g(\mu ))\) with \(t(\mu )\ge 0\) the nonnegative root of a quadratic depending continuously on \(\mu \)) would be a continuous retraction \(\overline{\mathbb D}\to S^1\). No such retraction exists: composing with \(\theta \mapsto e^{i\theta }\) and the homotopy \((s,\theta )\mapsto \rho (se^{i\theta })\) would show that the identity loop of \(S^1\) (winding number \(1\)) is homotopic to a constant loop (winding number \(0\)), contradicting homotopy invariance of the winding number / \(\pi _1(S^1)\cong \mathbb {Z}\). Reference: e.g. Milnor, Topology from the differentiable viewpoint, or any algebraic topology text.

Remark 2.42 Avoiding Brouwer
#

The only use of lemma 2.41 is to solve the scalar (two real dimensional) equation \(\mathcal{K}_\epsilon (\mu )=\mu \) on \(\overline{B}\). If one had Lipschitz dependence on \(\mu \) with small constant, namely

\[ \| \mathcal{G}_{\epsilon ,\mu }(w)-\mathcal{G}_{\epsilon ,\nu }(w)\| _{Y_{\gamma _1}}\le C\epsilon |\mu -\nu |\qquad (\mu ,\nu \in \overline{B},\ w\in \mathcal{B}_\epsilon ) \]

(which would require Lipschitz-in-\(\mu \) versions, into \(Y_{\gamma _1}\) with constant \(O(\epsilon )\), of theorem 2.20(4) for \(\mu \mapsto \mathcal{V}_\epsilon ^{-1}\mathcal{E}_\epsilon (\overline{f}_{\epsilon ,\mu })-G_\mu \) and of theorem 2.24(3); these are not established here), then the two-norm argument of lemma 2.36 gives \(\| w_{\epsilon ,\mu }-w_{\epsilon ,\nu }\| _{X_{\gamma _1}}\le C'\epsilon |\mu -\nu |\), hence \(\mathcal{K}_\epsilon \) would be \(C''\epsilon \)-Lipschitz and lemma 2.32 on \(\overline{B}\) would replace Brouwer (and give uniqueness of \(\mu _\epsilon \)). Note that \(\Pi G_\mu =0\) (lemma 2.16(4)), so the non-small \(\mu \)-dependence of \(G_\mu \) does not enter \(\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon ,\mu }\), and in \(\mathcal{K}_\epsilon \) it enters only through the \(O(\epsilon )\) difference \(\mathcal{V}_\epsilon ^{-1}\mathcal{E}_\epsilon (\overline{f}_{\epsilon ,\mu })-G_\mu \). Alternatively, a formal proof of the 2D Brouwer theorem is a self-contained project.
