11 Beyond the paper: the interior fractional Schauder estimate (cited in §4)

This chapter supplies a direct one-dimensional route to lemma 3.26. The target agrees with Proposition 2.2 of the arXiv version of [ ROS14 ] , with \(n=1\) and \(s=p/2\); Corollary 2.4 there is the related weighted-tail variant. The route below uses annular Fourier kernels and a cutoff commutator. It does not require formalizing the full boundary regularity theory.

Throughout, \(0{\lt}p{\lt}1\), and \(\beta {\gt}0\) and \(s=\beta +p\) are nonintegers unless stated otherwise. Write \(N_a(u;I)=\| u\| _{C^a(I)}\) for the norm of definition 3.1, \(N_a(u)=N_a(u;\mathbb {R})\), and \(k_a=\lfloor a\rfloor \), \(\theta _a=a-k_a\in (0,1)\). Global derivatives are ordinary derivatives; interval norms use derivatives within the interval. All integrals below are Lebesgue integrals. Finiteness must be established before using the real-valued Lean norm obtained with ENNReal.toReal. Constants may depend on the displayed exponents, derivative orders and the fixed cutoffs, but not on the input functions, dyadic index, or spatial variables.

Lemma 11.2 Cutoff and annular-symbol properties

Let \(a=k+\theta {\gt}0\), \(k\in \mathbb {N}\), \(0{\lt}\theta {\lt}1\). 11.2 Local finiteness and the commutator

Lemma 11.8 Near-part smoothness without bounds at infinity
✓
#

Uses

  • Definition 11.1
  • Definition 1.5

Lean declarations

  • GSQG.c12_frac_near_smooth

Let \(0{\lt}p{\lt}1\) and \(w\in C^\infty (\mathbb {R})\), without global derivative bounds. The function \(U(x)=c_p\int \chi (z)(w(x)-w(x-z))|z|^{-1-p}dz\) is smooth. Write \(w^{(m)}(x)-w^{(m)}(x-z)=z\int _0^1w^{(m+1)}(x-tz)dt\). The resulting majorant is a constant times \(\mathbf1_{|z|\le 2}|z|^{-p}\). Apply dominated differentiation inductively; the point \(z=0\) is assigned value zero.

Lemma 11.9 Far-part smoothness by differentiating the kernel

Let \(0{\lt}p{\lt}1\), and let \(w\) be smooth and bounded on \(\mathbb {R}\). Put \(\kappa _p(z)=(1-\chi (z))|z|^{-1-p}\), with value zero near the origin. Every derivative of \(\kappa _p\) is integrable. The far part is

\[ \| \partial ^m(K_{p,j}*h)\| _\infty \le C_{p,\beta ,m}2^{(m-\beta -p)j}N_\beta (h). \]
Proof ▶

Differentiate the Schwartz kernel, using boundedness of \(h\) and local translation majorants. Subtract the Taylor polynomial of \(h(x-z)\) under the integral; every polynomial term vanishes by the kernel moments. Bound the remainder and use the exact weighted scaling. No additional derivatives of \(h\) are required.

Lemma 11.23 Uniform reconstruction, including differentiated partial sums
✓
#

Uses

  • Definition 11.1
  • Lemma 11.14
  • Lemma 11.15

  • GSQG.c12_reconstruction_derivatives

Let \(v\in C_c^\infty (\mathbb {R};\mathbb {R})\) and \(P_nv=S_0v+\sum _{j=0}^{n-1}\Delta _jv\). For every integer \(m\ge 0\), \(\partial ^mP_nv\to \partial ^mv\) uniformly on \(\mathbb {R}\) as \(n\to \infty \).

Proof ▶

The differentiated partial sum is the real inverse transform of \((i\xi )^m\chi (2^{-n}\xi )\widehat v(\xi )\). Dominated convergence in \(L^1_\xi \) follows from the Schwartz decay of \(\widehat v\). The inverse-transform \(L^1\to L^\infty \) bound and Fourier inversion give the conclusion. This compact-input argument avoids building a general theorem on differentiating infinite series.

Lemma 11.24 The scalar dyadic increment estimate
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • GSQG.c12_dyadic_increment_sum

For every \(0{\lt}\theta {\lt}1\) there is \(C_\theta {\gt}0\) such that for every \(d\ge 0\) and \(n\in \mathbb {N}\),

\[ \sum _{j=0}^{n-1}\min \{ 2^{-\theta j},d\, 2^{(1-\theta )j}\} \le C_\theta d^\theta . \]

For every \(a{\gt}0\), \(\sum _{j\ge 0}2^{-aj}\) converges, with sum \((1-2^{-a})^{-1}\).

Proof ▶

If \(d=0\) the first sum is zero; if \(d\ge 1\) use the decreasing geometric series. For \(0{\lt}d{\lt}1\), choose \(J\in \mathbb {N}\) with \(2^Jd\le 1{\lt}2^{J+1}d\). Sum the increasing geometric expression for \(j\le J\) and the decreasing one for \(j{\gt}J\). Both are bounded by a constant times \(d^\theta \), uniformly in the partial-sum cutoff.

Lemma 11.25 Compact-input global Schauder estimate
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • GSQG.c12_compact_schauder

Let \(0{\lt}p{\lt}1\), \(\beta {\gt}0\), and assume \(\beta \) and \(s=\beta +p\) are nonintegers. There is \(C_{p,\beta }{\gt}0\) such that, for every real \(v\in C_c^\infty (\mathbb {R})\) with \(h=|D|^pv\in C^\beta (\mathbb {R})\),

\[ v\in C^{\beta +p}(\mathbb {R}),\qquad N_{\beta +p}(v)\le C_{p,\beta }\bigl(\| v\| _\infty +N_\beta (h)\bigr). \]
Proof ▶

Put \(k=\lfloor s\rfloor \), \(\theta =s-k\). For \(0\le m\le k\), sum the block bounds \(2^{(m-s)j}\) and pass to the uniformly reconstructed derivative. For the \(k\)th derivative increment, each block is bounded by a constant times \(N_\beta (h)\min (2^{-\theta j},|x-y|2^{(1-\theta )j})\). Use the scalar increment lemma and pass from finite sums to \(v^{(k)}\) by uniform convergence. The low block is controlled by its \(k\)th and \((k+1)\)st derivative bounds, splitting \(|x-y|\le 1\) from \(|x-y|\ge 1\). Assemble the finite Holder norm with the norm constructors. The \((k+1)\)st derivative block estimate is used only for increments: its infinite series is not claimed to be summable.

11.6 Assembly of the estimate

For lemma 3.26, take \(v=\eta w\). Lemma 11.13 supplies the global \(C^\beta \) norm of \(|D|^pv\) and the value bound for \(v\). Apply lemma 11.25, then restrict to \([-1/2,1/2]\) using lemma 11.3. Since \(\eta =1\) on a neighborhood of this interval, the restriction agrees with \(w\), including its within derivatives. Absorb the two fixed constants.
